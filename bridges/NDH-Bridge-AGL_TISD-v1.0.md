# ⭐ **1 — Purpose of the AGL–TISD Bridge**

This bridge document defines the **formal relationship** between:

- **AGL v2.0 (Accessibility Geometry Layer)**  
- **TISD (Trauma‑Informed Systems Design)**  

It ensures:

- no trauma geometry contamination  
- no accessibility geometry over‑assertion  
- no altitude shear  
- no runtime overload  
- no relational collapse  
- no tone instability  
- no expressive drift  

It is a **stabilization bridge**, not a directive.

---

# ⭐ **2 — Why This Bridge Is Required**

AGL v2.0 stabilizes:

- sensory load  
- runtime load  
- relational load  
- tone load  
- expressive load  

TISD stabilizes:

- trauma geometry  
- harm loops  
- tensor distortion  
- holonomy collapse  
- trauma‑safe operators  

These two geometries must **interlock**, but **never merge**.

Without a bridge:

- AGL could accidentally descend into trauma geometry  
- TISD could accidentally absorb accessibility geometry  
- TIDS could collapse under mixed load  
- Zen‑Bridge could destabilize  
- Constellation could drift  
- Research‑Pilot could overload  

This bridge prevents those failure modes.

# ⭐ **6 — Bridge Rules (Governance‑Adjacent)**

### **Rule 1 — AGL must not descend into trauma geometry.**  
No interaction with trauma operators, trauma basins, trauma tensors, or STAR‑12.

### **Rule 2 — TISD must not absorb accessibility geometry.**  
No migration of AGL operators into trauma geometry.

### **Rule 3 — All interactions must pass through the bridge.**  
No direct AGL ↔ TISD contact.

### **Rule 4 — AGL stabilizes load before trauma geometry is evaluated.**  
AGL envelopes must be applied first.

### **Rule 5 — TISD stabilizes trauma geometry after AGL envelopes.**  
Trauma geometry must be evaluated second.

### **Rule 6 — No directive propagation until the Directive Index exists.**  
AGL and TISD remain directive‑adjacent, not directive‑generative.
